# The Sound of Alpha RoundUp Part 2
*Album identity statement — one page, governs every generation. (Chairman addition, Executive Review. Draft for EP ratification.)*

**What makes this soundtrack unique:** it was composed *from the riders' own testimony*. Seventy-five people answered one question, and seventeen of them independently said "therapy." The score's melodic and lyrical DNA comes from a governed registry of their actual words — not from a composer's guess about biker culture. No other soundtrack can claim its themes were extracted, cited, and consent-governed before a note was written.

**What listeners should immediately recognize as The Workman Experience:** warmth with horsepower. Road-soul — live-band feel, real drums, gritty-but-clean guitar, gospel undertones — where the engines are treated as instruments, not noise to duck. Confidence without aggression. Reverence without solemnity. The sound of being *welcomed* into a brotherhood, because that is what the footage shows.

**Genres welcome:** soul, blues-rock, gospel, funk, Southern hip-hop color (as accent, especially night grooves), Americana texture. **Genres off-limits:** EDM festival drops · pop-punk · trap-metal aggression · horror/tension scoring · ironic or novelty treatments · generic royalty-free "epic cinematic."

**Emotional colors that dominate:** pride, warmth, gratitude, momentum, joy, remembrance. **Colors that never appear:** menace, self-pity, mockery, manufactured drama.

**What should never appear:** music over the two silence zones or the R46 carve-out · lyrics quoting an identifiable person without consent clearance · mickey-mousing the visuals · a fade-out on the final cue (the film ends on a held resolve, cut to black) · any sound that makes the riders characters in someone else's story. This is their story; the music works for them.
