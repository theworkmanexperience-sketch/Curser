Next Steps in Final Cut Pro

W.E. C.A.P.E. — your shoot is exported and importing. Follow these in order.

Everything is already time-stamped, grouped by camera, and set to the right format. You just point Final Cut at it — the steps below take about two minutes, once.
1

Finish the import

When Final Cut asks, click Replace (updates the media) and Keep as Standard (SDR). Your shoot shows up as an Event in the left sidebar.

2

Put everything in shooting order — do this once

In the browser (middle panel), switch to List view (the list icon, top-left), then click the “Name” column heading.

Every clip and photo is named with the time it was shot, so Name order = first taken → last taken, across all cameras. Final Cut remembers this — you only set it once.

3

Open your ready-made timeline

Under the Event, double-click the project named “… — Edit”. It's already the right size and frame rate — no setup questions. Just start dropping clips onto it.

4

Find each camera instantly

In the sidebar under the Event, you'll see collections like Camera: DJI Osmo Action 6, Camera: Insta360 X5, and Stills (your photos & screenshots).

Click one → only that camera's clips, already in order. Select them and drag onto the timeline. No scrolling through everything.

5

Use the synced moments (Multicam)

Clips named “Multicam 01 / 02 …” are moments where two or more cameras were rolling together, bundled as one. Double-click one → the Angle Editor → click between cameras to cut live.

Aligned to within a second or two by time. To lock it to the frame, select the clips and use Clip ▸ Synchronize Clips (it fine-tunes with the audio).

6

It stays fast (proxies)

You're editing on lightweight proxy copies, so it's smooth even if the big footage drive isn't plugged in. For your final export, connect the originals drive and switch the Viewer's View ▸ Optimized/Original.

7

Color-code by camera (optional)

Each clip is tagged with its camera as a Role. Open Timeline Index ▸ Roles to color, filter, or focus one camera at a time while you edit.

If a clip looks black or says “Missing”

The media drive just isn't connected. Plug in your footage drives (the 10TB originals and WE_CAPE_OUTPUT proxies), then in Final Cut choose File ▸ Relink Files and point it at the folder.

A clip or photo sitting in the wrong spot on the timeline usually means that camera's clock was set wrong — the clip's name shows the true time W.E. C.A.P.E. recovered, so sorting by Name still lines it up.

W.E. C.A.P.E. — Capture every moment. Miss nothing.
