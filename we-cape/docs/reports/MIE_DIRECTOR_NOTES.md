## Documentary Thesis
Riding is not recreation here — it is therapy, brotherhood, and
inheritance. Seventy-five riders answer one question and converge,
unprompted, on the same three words. The film's job is to let that
convergence become undeniable.

## Emotional Arc (five progressions, ETC-anchored)
P1 ARRIVAL (opens on the Jun-25 OM-1 huddle): intimate, unscored or
   near-silent — earn the scale before announcing it.
P2 GAUNTLET ONE (staging interviews, ~02–27min): rhythmic, building;
   music SUPPORTS under speech, never leads; the Q1-Q5 cadence is
   itself percussive.
P3 THE RIDE & THE TOWN (escort → librarian → proclamations,
   ~27–39min): music LEADS the escort sequence (the film's first
   full-energy statement), then falls away entirely — the mayor's
   proclamation and the state reading play dry; civic words carry
   their own weight.
P4 GAUNTLET TWO + HONORS (~39–54min): warmer return of the interview
   rhythm; at the honors segment and the moment of silence (~53:30
   region) — ABSOLUTE SILENCE, the film's one mandatory quiet.
P5 BIKE NIGHT → WRAP (evening arrivals through the triple-080 close):
   music LEADS; the last 62 seconds (three cuts of clip 080) are the
   victory statement — one cue, unbroken, carrying to black and the
   Part 3 tease.

## Where Music Leads / Supports / Yields
LEADS: escort ride · high-energy ride sequences · bike-night arrivals ·
  the 080 closing run · any montage built on the Why-I-Ride spine.
SUPPORTS (ducked, low information density): both interview gauntlets ·
  organizer thanks · committee segments.
SILENCE PREFERRED: R46's PTSD disclosure (therapy theme at its most
  exposed — score would cheapen it) · the moment of silence · both
  proclamations · the first-time-rider beat.

## Intended Audience Experience
Arrive curious, leave understanding: by the final cue the viewer should
hear "wind therapy" as literal. The soundtrack's covenant — never
compete with a rider's voice; spend energy on the road, reverence on
the words.
